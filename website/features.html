Recording

  • One record button

    Your camera and mic start together, and everything stops together.

  • Starts on your face

    Every take starts on your camera. Share your screen whenever you are ready.

  • A 3, 2, 1 first

    A countdown runs before anything records, so you start talking at the beep. Press the button again to call the take off.

  • Me and Screen

    One click switches the video between your camera filling the screen and your screen. Your camera grows out of the face circle and shrinks back into it.

  • Your face in the video

    Put your face over the screen as a circle, square, oval or wide shape, or leave it out.

  • Back to the recorder

    During a take, bring the app's window up over what you are sharing. The take keeps going, and the window never shows in the video.

  • Framed for you

    While the screen shows, a small box shows your face framed like a camera operator would: still while you talk, a smooth glide when you move. It stays on your screen, never in the video.

Your screen

  • The whole screen or one window

    Pick from pictures of your screens and windows. Your last pick comes up already picked.

  • Windows on other desktops

    Share a window that is full screen on another desktop, like Chrome. Picking it moves the Mac there.

  • Follows one window

    Sharing one window records only that window, even when something covers it, and the face circle moves with it.

  • The app stays out of the video

    The recording box and the app's other windows never appear in the recording.

  • The Mac's sound, your way

    Record sound from every app, from one app only (like a video in Chrome), or not at all, and change it at any moment of the take.

Cameras and phones

  • Any camera

    Your Mac's camera, a USB webcam, a real camera through an HDMI to USB capture card, or an iPhone through Continuity Camera.

  • Phones over Wi-Fi

    Scan a QR code with an iPhone or an Android phone and it films over your Wi-Fi, with no app and no cable, at up to 1080p and 30 frames a second.

  • Up to four phones

    Film from up to four phones at once, each from its own code and each saved as its own file.

  • Wide or tall

    Each phone films Wide 16:9 for YouTube or Tall 9:16 for Shorts and Reels, and keeps that shape however it turns.

  • Phones rest between takes

    Between takes a phone shows its own picture so you can frame the shot, but sends nothing to the Mac unless the Mac is showing its picture, which saves its battery.

  • Every angle lines up

    Each extra camera is saved with the same sound as the main one, so all the angles line up on their own.

Microphones

  • Any microphone

    A USB mic, a wireless kit's receiver, AirPods, your Mac's own mic, or a phone. AirPods record at phone call quality, and the app tells you so.

  • Several at once

    Every mic records its own file and has its own level meter while you set up.

  • Pick the video's sound

    Choose which mic the finished video uses. The others still record.

  • A filming phone's own sound

    A phone that films an angle can record its own mic too, as its own file.

  • A phone as a wireless mic

    Scan a code with a spare phone, tap Start microphone, and keep it about a hand's width from your mouth.

  • One mute switch

    Mute a phone's mic from the phone or from the Mac. Muted, the mic is fully off, and its file stays in step.

  • Hear every mic

    While watching a take, play any mic under any camera to hear which sounds best.

After you stop

  • Every file lined up

    The camera, screen and mic files are lined up by their sound.

  • The finished video

    For a take with your screen, video.mp4 is what was on the screen, with your camera filling it whenever you clicked Me.

  • A transcript

    words.json has every word you said with its time. It needs a free speech model, see How to use it.

  • YouTube chapters

    chapters.txt is ready to paste into a YouTube description.

  • Your retakes

    Say "retake" and repeat the line. retakes.json lists every retake with its time.

  • Smaller camera files

    The camera file loses its empty padding, about a third of its size, with no change to the picture or the sound.

  • A plain report

    report.md sums up the take in plain words.

  • Quick videos

    Switch off Transcript and chapters in Settings when a video only needs the files.

Your recordings

  • Every take with a picture

    See every take with a picture, its length and its size.

  • Watch in the app

    Switch between the finished video, the camera and the screen at the same moment of the take.

  • Search and filter

    Search by name, and filter by This week, Not finished, With screen or Camera only.

  • Rename, copy or delete

    Rename a take, save a copy, or delete it, which moves it to the Trash.

Watching live

  • From any browser

    Watch the shoot from another laptop or phone: every camera, the screen while recording, the mic level and the checks. No login.

  • Listen

    Hear the mic as it is recorded, about a quarter of a second late, on headphones.

  • Pin and full screen

    Make any camera or the screen the big picture, or fill the screen with it.

  • Home Wi-Fi or Anywhere

    It works on your Wi-Fi. Anywhere works from any internet connection, through a free Cloudflare tunnel.

  • A secret link

    The link is the only key. New link stops every old link working.

Checks and settings

  • Checks before you start

    Camera, mic, screen, disk space and battery, each with a plain fix when something is wrong.

  • A Low Power Mode warning

    It tells you when Low Power Mode is on, because that can make a take stutter.

  • Light mode for smaller Macs

    With 8 GB of memory or less, or in Low Power Mode, the camera stays at 1080p and 30 frames a second.

  • Settings in plain words

    Every setting says in one sentence what it does.

  • A guide to connecting cameras

    Steps for an iPhone, a webcam or a real camera, with a list of the cameras your Mac sees right now.

Safe, light, private

  • Saved every 2 seconds

    The files are written in 2-second pieces, so a crash keeps the take.

  • A camera that stops is caught

    If the camera stops sending pictures, the take stops within about 6 seconds and says why.

  • Light on your Mac

    About 7% of one processor core between takes on a MacBook Air. When you switch to another app, the camera turns off after about 20 seconds and wakes when you come back.

  • Private

    Your recordings and transcripts never leave your Mac. The live view reaches beyond your Wi-Fi only if you switch on Anywhere.

All of this, free.

No account, no subscription, no watermark and no time limit. The code is on GitHub.
