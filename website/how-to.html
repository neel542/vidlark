How to use it

Install it once from GitHub, then every video is one press of the record button. Everything here is free, including the tools it needs.

On a Mac

Vidlark is free. So are Xcode, Homebrew and every tool in the steps by hand.

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon on macOS 15 or later.
  • The Xcode app from the App Store, for the free certificate in step 3.
  • Start the Xcode download first: it is the largest part of the install.
  • For a phone camera or phone mic: the phone and the Mac on the same Wi-Fi.

Install

Vidlark is built on your own Mac from its code on GitHub. Claude Code can do the whole install for you, or you can follow the steps by hand. Both end with the same app.

With Claude Code

Claude Code is Anthropic's coding assistant, in the Claude desktop app or in Terminal. It needs a paid Claude plan; the steps by hand cost nothing. Paste this into Claude Code and answer its questions.

One step stays yours: adding your Apple ID in Xcode for the free certificate. Claude tells you where to click, waits for you, then builds the app again.

By hand

Open the Terminal app and follow these steps in order.

  1. Install Xcode's command line tools. If they are already installed, Terminal says so.

    xcode-select --install

  2. Install Homebrew, then the two tools that finish each take. ffmpeg makes the finished video, and whisper-cpp writes the transcript.

    /bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"
    brew install ffmpeg whisper-cpp

  3. Get a free Apple Development certificate. macOS only lets an app record the screen when it has one. In Xcode, open Settings, then Accounts, add your Apple ID, then Manage Certificates, the + button, and Apple Development.

  4. Download the code.

    git clone https://github.com/neel542/vidlark.git
cd vidlark

  5. Build the app and open it. The build finds your certificate on its own.

    bash Tools/build.sh
open "dist/Vidlark.app"

  6. For transcripts, download the speech model (about 550 MB). Without it, everything records and only the transcript and the retakes list are skipped.

    mkdir -p ~/.cache/whisper
curl -L -o ~/.cache/whisper/ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin https://huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/resolve/main/ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin

The first time

  1. Allow the camera and the microphone when macOS asks.

  2. Allow screen recording. When the app asks, switch on Vidlark in System Settings, under Privacy and Security, Screen and System Audio Recording. Then quit the app and open it again.

Your first video

  1. Check the Sources list. It shows your camera, mic and screen, each with a lamp. Green means ready. Click a row to change it.

  2. Press the red record button. It counts down 3, 2, 1 with beeps. Start talking at the higher beep: that is when recording starts.

  3. Share your screen when you want it in the video. Press Share screen, pick the entire screen or one window, then Share. The window shrinks to a small box with Me, Screen and the stop button.

  4. Click Me or Screen to switch what the video shows. Your camera grows to fill the screen, or shrinks back into the circle.

  5. Press stop. The app lines up the files and, if you shared your screen, makes the finished video. Open Recordings (Command-Shift-R) to watch it.

Made a mistake? Say "retake" and repeat the line. The take keeps going, and retakes.json lists where every retake is.

Add a phone camera

  1. Press + Add, then Add a phone with a QR code. A window shows a QR code.

  2. Point the phone's camera at the code and tap the link that appears. It works with an iPhone or an Android phone, with no app to install.

  3. Get past the one-time warning. The phone says the connection is not private, because the link is the app's own, on your Wi-Fi only. On an iPhone, tap Show Details, then visit this website, then Visit Website. On Android, tap Advanced, then Proceed.

  4. Pick Wide 16:9 or Tall 9:16, then tap Start camera. The window on the Mac says Connected.

Each phone records its own file, camera-2.mov and on, lined up with the main camera by sound. Add up to four phones the same way.

Add a microphone

  1. Plug in the mic or connect it, then press + Add and pick it under Another microphone.

  2. For a phone as a wireless mic, pick A phone as a microphone, with a QR code. Scan it, tap Start microphone, then Allow, and keep the phone about a hand's width from your mouth.

  3. To use that mic in the finished video, open its row's menu and pick Use for the video's sound.

Every mic records its own file, mic-2.m4a and on, whichever one the video uses.

Watch live from another device

  1. Open Settings (the gear, or Command-Comma), then Live view, and pick Home Wi-Fi.

  2. Press Copy link and open it in a browser on another laptop or phone on the same Wi-Fi. There is no login.

  3. For other networks, pick Anywhere. It needs one more free tool:

    brew install cloudflared

Keep the link private. Anyone with it sees your screen. New link stops every old link working.

Where your files go

Every take gets its own folder:

/Users/Shared/Vidlark Recordings/<date> <title>/recording-1/
FileWhat it is
video.mp4The finished video, made when you share your screen.
camera.movYour camera, with your voice.
screen.movYour screen, with your voice and the Mac's sound if it was on.
camera-2.movEach extra camera or phone.
mic-2.m4aEach extra microphone.
mic.wavThe clean voice track.
words.jsonThe transcript, with the time of every word.
chapters.txtYouTube chapters to paste into a description.
retakes.jsonEvery "retake", with its time.
report.mdA plain summary of the take.

On Windows

Vidlark for Windows is free. The tools that finish each take come inside the download.

Coming soon. Vidlark for Windows is being built now. These steps are ready for the day its download goes up on GitHub.

What you need

  • A PC with Windows 11, 64-bit.
  • A webcam and a microphone, built in or plugged in.
  • Nothing else to install: ffmpeg and the speech tool come inside the download.

Download and open

  1. Download Vidlark for Windows. It is one zip file from GitHub.

    Download: coming soon

  2. Unzip it. Right-click the zip, choose Extract All, then Extract. Keep the folder together: the app and its tools sit side by side in it.

  3. Open Vidlark.exe in that folder.

  4. Get past the one-time warning. Windows says "Windows protected your PC", because the app is new and not signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

The first time

  1. Check that apps may use the camera and the mic. If the picture or the level meter stays empty, open Settings, then Privacy and security, then Camera, and switch on Let desktop apps access your camera. Do the same under Microphone.

Your first video

  1. Type a title and check your camera and mic. Each has a lamp. Green means ready. Pick another one from its list to change it.

  2. Press the red record button. It counts down 3, 2, 1 with beeps. Start talking at the higher beep: that is when recording starts.

  3. Press stop. Vidlark lines up the files. Press Show the take to open its folder.

Where your files go

Every take gets its own folder, with the same files as on a Mac:

C:\Users\Public\Videos\Vidlark Recordings\<date> <title>\recording-1\
