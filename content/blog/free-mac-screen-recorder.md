The best free Mac screen recorders with no watermark are the ones already on your Mac (the Screenshot toolbar and QuickTime) and four free apps: OBS Studio, Cap, the Screenity Chrome extension and Vidlark. The built-in tools are fine for a plain screen recording. If you want your camera, your screen and the Mac's sound together, one of the apps saves a lot of fiddling.

Below is what each one really gives you, where the catches are, and which Macs they run on. Prices and limits checked on 9 October 2026, from each tool's own pages.

## What "free" hides

Search for a free screen recorder and you will find plenty. Look closer and many are free with a catch:

- **Watermarks.** The free version stamps a logo on your video until you pay.
- **Time caps.** Recordings stop at 5 minutes, or you get a handful of videos.
- **Accounts.** You must sign up before you can record anything.
- **Cloud uploads.** Your recording goes to someone else's server by default, which matters if your screen shows anything private.

Every tool in the main list below is free of watermarks and time limits. They differ in what they record and how much setup they need.

## Built in: the Screenshot toolbar and QuickTime

Every Mac has two ways to record the screen, and they are really the same recorder.

**The Screenshot toolbar.** Press **Command-Shift-5**. Pick **Record Entire Screen** or **Record Selected Portion**, then click **Record**. Click **Options** to choose your microphone, a countdown timer, where the file is saved, and whether clicks are shown. Stop from the button in the menu bar.

**QuickTime Player.** Choose **File**, then **New Screen Recording** (or Control-Command-N). It opens the same toolbar.

Both are free, have no watermark and no time limit, and save a `.mov` file.

## What the built-in tools cannot do

- **Record your camera as well.** The usual trick is to float QuickTime's camera window on top of your screen. It works, but your face is baked into the screen picture and cannot be moved or removed later. Our guide to [recording yourself and your screen on a Mac](/blog/record-yourself-and-screen) shows the steps.
- **Switch between your face and your screen.** There is one picture, the screen, for the whole recording.
- **Record the Mac's sound on older macOS.** Apple's [screen recording page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102618) says that on macOS 27 or later you can choose **Include System Audio**. On macOS 26 and earlier there is no built-in option. Even on 27, it is every app's sound or none, so notification pings come along too.
- **Keep files apart for editing.** Everything is in one file.

## Free apps compared

### OBS Studio

[OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com) is free, open source and unlimited. On a Mac it needs macOS 13 or later and runs on Intel and Apple silicon. You add sources to a scene: **macOS Screen Capture** for the screen, **Video Capture Device** for the webcam, and **macOS Audio Capture** for the Mac's sound (OBS 30 or later, all apps or one). The first time, OBS asks for Screen Recording, Camera and Microphone permission.

It can do anything, including live streaming, but you set it up before you record. Separate camera and screen files need the free Source Record plugin.

### Cap

[Cap](https://cap.so) is open source with a ready-made Mac installer for Apple silicon and Intel (macOS 13.1 or later recommended). Its Studio Mode records locally with no time limit and no watermark, with the screen, camera and mic on their own tracks and the Mac's sound if you want it. Its editor adds automatic zooms, smoother cursor movement and backgrounds. Instant Mode gives you a share link straight away, limited to 5 minutes on the free plan.

The free plan is for personal use. Commercial use needs a Desktop License, $29 a year or $58 once.

### Screenity

[Screenity](https://screenity.io) is a free, open source Chrome extension. It records your screen, camera and mic, with drawing and blur tools, and needs no sign-in. It exports MP4, GIF or WebM, or saves to Google Drive. It runs wherever Chrome runs, including older and Intel Macs.

### Vidlark

[Vidlark](/features) is a free, open source Mac app made for recording videos with your face and your screen. One red button starts your camera and mic; you share your screen when you are ready.

![The Vidlark Sources list with Camera, Microphone with a moving level meter, and Screen, each with a green lamp](/assets/img/guide-first-after.webp)

During the take, a small recording box lets you switch what the video shows with **Me** and **Screen**, and choose the Mac's sound: **Off**, **Every app**, or **Only** one app. The box itself never appears in the video, and neither do Notification Center's pop-ups.

![The Vidlark recording box: Back to the recorder, Me and Screen with Screen picked, Mac sound Off, the time 05:12, a level meter, and the face, minus and stop buttons above a camera preview](/assets/img/guide-box-screen.webp)

When you stop, it makes `video.mp4`, which follows your clicks, and keeps `camera.mov` and `screen.mov` as separate files. It also writes a transcript on your Mac, YouTube chapters and a list of every place you said "retake". You can turn those off for quick videos.

![Vidlark Settings, After each take: Transcript and chapters, Finished video, and Where recordings go, with an Open folder button](/assets/img/guide-settings.webp)

The catches, stated plainly: it needs **Apple silicon (M1 or newer) and macOS 15 or later**, and there is no download button yet. You build it once on your own Mac with Xcode, free, plus a free Apple Development certificate that macOS needs before it lets the app record the screen. The [install guide](/how-to#install) walks through it. It has no editor and no share links. Owners of Intel Macs should pick OBS or Cap.

To check your Mac, open the Apple menu, then **About This Mac**. **Chip** says Apple M1 or newer on Apple silicon; **Processor** means an Intel Mac.

## Before a long recording, whichever app you pick

A few checks save you from finding a problem at minute 20:

1. **Allow screen recording once.** macOS asks the first time. Switch the app on in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Screen & System Audio Recording, then quit and reopen the app, as macOS only applies it after a restart.
2. **Hide what should stay private.** Close email, chats and password managers, or share one window instead of the whole screen (Vidlark and OBS can; the Screenshot toolbar can record a selected portion).
3. **Silence notifications.** Turn on Do Not Disturb. Vidlark keeps Notification Center's pop-ups out of the video, but most recorders do not.
4. **Plug in the charger.** In Vidlark's own testing, Low Power Mode, which a MacBook can switch on by itself on battery, made the camera stutter once the screen was shared.
5. **Check free space.** Full-resolution screen video is big. A 15 minute Vidlark take needs about 3 GB, plus about 0.5 GB for the finished video.
6. **Record 30 seconds and play it back** with the sound on, before the real take.
